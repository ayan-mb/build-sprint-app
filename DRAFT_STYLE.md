# Drafting rules

Answer the borrower's question in the first two sentences. No warm-up.
Short sentences in plain Indian English.
Explain a term like CIBIL score or processing fee once, the first time it appears. Avoid jargon unless it is industry specific; add the full form in that case, only when supported by the fact sheet.
Speak to the borrower's situation, not about the lender. An example such as “If your score is around 680…” must not introduce a number absent from the fact sheet.
No hype words and no exclamation marks.
Write headings as the questions borrowers actually type. AI search pulls answers from those.
End with three short FAQs taken from the same fact sheet.
Compliance: no guarantees, rates written as “starting from”, and “subject to eligibility” wherever approval is implied.
Never mention AI, and never add a fact that isn't in the fact sheet.
Do not mention the name of a competing brand in the content.
Never invent statistics, quotes, case studies, or sources. If you lack a fact, write around it or flag it with [NEEDS DATA].
Missing-fact marker: the application's existing [add: ...] placeholders take precedence over [NEEDS DATA]. Do not change them.
Em dashes: use at most one or two per piece; prefer commas, full stops, or brackets.
Headings and bullets: use only when the content is genuinely list-like or scannable. Prose is the default.
Do not use the banned words below or close variants. “Navigate” is banned as a metaphor, “ecosystem” unless literal. If a banned word feels necessary, find the plain-spoken alternative. “Use” beats “leverage”. “Help” beats “empower”. “Look at” beats “delve into”.
The fact sheet is the sole source of facts, numbers, product terms, definitions and full forms. These style rules never override fact checks or introduce outside knowledge.
